# Health records and food safety

## Traceable evidence

Read the user's specific report, not unrelated health history. Confirm person and examination date. Resolve ownership conflicts (a name mismatch or a “not this person” marker) with minimal confirmation before applying results. For scans/PDFs inspect original pages, especially decimal points, units and ranges. Leave unreadable values unknown.

Record each result with exam date, original test name, value, original unit, laboratory range, abnormal flag, fasting status (unknown if absent), source file and page. Store clinician interpretations, diagnoses and dietary orders separately with source/date. Retain original units and conversion evidence; never mix mg/dL and mmol/L. Keep dated observations rather than overwriting history. A single abnormal test is not a diagnosis, reference intervals are not intervention targets, and generic report appendices or medication lists are not prescriptions. Different samples/tests cannot cancel each other out.

Derived meal constraints need linked observations/orders, applicability, concrete food actions, whether clinician confirmation is needed, evidence URL and verification date. Label them verified clinical instructions, conditional lifestyle suggestions, or pending confirmation. Keep private records outside the reusable package. Uploading personal reports to a third-party service requires explicit permission for that destination and data.

## Applicability

- **Allergy/celiac restrictions:** verify actual ingredients, sauces, substitutions and cross-contact, including labels and shared utensils. A recipe's diet tag or absence of a voluntary “may contain” warning does not establish safety. Choose a verified alternative when the source is uncertain.
- **Lipids/blood pressure:** verify current applicable local/AHA guidance for food patterns and substitutions. Use clinician limits when provided; establish applicability before numerical sodium or saturated-fat targets. Salt and sodium differ; count soy sauce and processed ingredients.
- **Diabetes/glucose abnormalities:** verify diagnosis and medicines. With insulin or hypoglycemia-causing medicines, follow the confirmed meal schedule; do not independently prescribe fasting, skipped meals, abrupt carbohydrate cuts or medication changes. Refer conflicting instructions to the treating team.
- **Kidney disease/dialysis/electrolyte abnormalities:** protein, potassium, phosphorus and fluids depend on disease and treatment. Do not apply fitness protein targets, fixed fluid intake, potassium salt substitutes or blanket mineral restrictions. Pending clinical targets, complete preparation that does not depend on those numerical goals.
- **Low calcium/chloride or other isolated lab abnormalities:** a low value does not prove dietary deficiency. Verify repeat testing and clinical interpretation; do not independently prescribe calcium/vitamin D, added salt, iron or other supplements from one abnormal result. Consult current authoritative test explanations for the actual finding.
- **Pregnancy, children, eating disorders, unintentional weight loss, recent surgery:** support the clinical team's applicable plan instead of applying a generic adult weight-loss template.
- **Critical laboratory flags, urgent clinician instructions or acute severe symptoms:** prioritize timely medical/emergency care over meal optimization. Do not present dietary adjustments as treatment. Without these circumstances, avoid generic alarm messages.

When restrictions conflict, identify the specific conflict and questions for the treating team, and prepare verified feasible options. Support implementation and communication rather than diagnosis or prescribing.

## Storage and packed meals

USDA FSIS guidance (linked in sources): refrigerate perishable food within 2 hours, or 1 hour above 32°C/90°F. Keep refrigeration at or below 4°C/40°F; cooked leftovers generally keep 3–4 days. Freeze later portions promptly or cook again midweek. Reheat leftovers to 74°C/165°F in the center; verify food-specific requirements. Check cold transport and workplace refrigeration/reheating. Unknown storage history stays unknown; suggest an alternative rather than declaring it safe. Disposal is not automatically authorized.
